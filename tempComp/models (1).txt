\section{System Model}
We consider an edge-assisted crowdsourcing system for video analytics in vehicular networks. The system involves a task requester (platform) and a pool of mobile workers (vehicles). We model the system operations in discrete time slots $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$.

\subsection{Task Modeling}
At each time slot $t$, a set of $M$ video analytics tasks arrives, denoted as $\mathcal{T}^t = \{ T_1^t, \dots, T_M^t \}$. Each task $T_j^t$ is characterized by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data Size ($D_j^t$):} The amount of video data to be processed, determined by the frame rate $f_j$ and resolution $R_j$, such that $D_j^t = f_j \cdot R_j$.
    \item \textbf{Quality Requirement ($Q_j^{\text{req}}$):} The minimum cumulative quality required to consider the task successfully completed.
    \item \textbf{Payment ($p_j$):} The unit price offered by the requester for the task's quality.
\end{itemize}

Tasks are assigned to a set of available workers $\mathcal{W}^t = \{ w_1^t, \dots, w_N^t \}$. We introduce a binary decision variable $x_{i,j}^t \in \{0, 1\}$ to represent the assignment:
\begin{equation}
x_{i,j}^t = 
\begin{cases} 
1, & \text{if worker } w_i^t \text{ is assigned to task } T_j^t, \\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Worker Modeling and Context}
Workers are heterogeneous vehicles equipped with processing and communication units. 

\subsubsection{Context Vector}
Each worker $w_i^t$ is associated with a $d$-dimensional context vector $\mathbf{z}_i^t \in \mathcal{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$, capturing dynamic attributes that influence task execution. These attributes include **driving speed**, **bandwidth**, **processor performance**, **physical location**, and environmental factors such as **weather**. 

To facilitate the partitioning of the context space $\mathcal{Z}$, all dimensions in $\mathbf{z}_i^t$ are normalized to a standard range (e.g., $[0, 1]$). The context space is adaptively partitioned into cells, where each cell represents a region of workers with similar performance characteristics.

\subsubsection{Energy Consumption Model}
Worker energy consumption consists of transmission and computation components.

\textbf{Transmission Energy:} The energy required for worker $w_i^t$ to download or upload data for task $T_j^t$ is:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{trans}, i,j}^t = \alpha_{\text{trans}} \cdot D_j^t \cdot \left( \frac{d_i}{d_0} \right)^\beta \cdot x_{i,j}^t,
\end{equation}
where $d_i$ is the transmission distance, $d_0$ is the reference distance, $\beta$ is the path loss exponent, and $\alpha_{\text{trans}}$ is the energy coefficient per data unit.

\textbf{Analysis Energy:} The computational energy required to process the video data is:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{analyze}, i, j}^t = \alpha_{\text{analyze}} \cdot P_{\text{total}, i}^t \cdot x_{i,j}^t.
\end{equation}
Here, $\alpha_{\text{analyze}}$ represents the energy per unit of processing power. The total power $P_{\text{total}, i}^t$ depends on the baseline power $P_{\text{base}, i}^t$ and the task complexity scaling factor $\alpha_{\text{task}, i}$, adjusted by the frame rate ratio $f_j / f_0$:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{total}, i}^t = \alpha_{\text{task}, i} \cdot P_{\text{base}, i}^t \cdot \frac{f_j}{f_0}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Total Energy:} The total energy consumption for worker $w_i^t$ at slot $t$ is the sum of energies for all assigned tasks (typically one per slot):
\begin{equation}
E_{i}^t = \sum_{j=1}^{M} \left( E_{\text{trans}, i,j}^t + E_{\text{analyze}, i,j}^t \right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Platform Modeling}
The platform aims to maximize profit by balancing revenue from completed tasks against incentives paid to workers.

\subsubsection{Revenue and Cost}
The platform revenue from task $T_j^t$ depends on the realized task quality $Q(\mathbf{z}_{i,j}^t)$, which is a function of the worker's context:
\begin{equation}
R_j^t = p_j \cdot Q(\mathbf{z}_{i,j}^t).
\end{equation}
To compensate workers for resource usage, the platform pays a cost $C_i^t$ proportional to the energy consumed, with a rate $\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
C_i^t = \lambda E_i^t.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Profit Maximization}
The net profit $\Pi^t$ at time slot $t$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\Pi^t = \sum_{j=1}^{M} R_j^t - \sum_{i=1}^{N} C_i^t.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Assumptions}
We make the following standard assumptions to facilitate theoretical analysis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(A1) Lipschitz Continuity:} The expected reward function $Q(\mathbf{z})$ is Lipschitz continuous with constant $L$, ensuring similar contexts yield similar rewards: $|Q(\mathbf{z}_i) - Q(\mathbf{z}_j)| \leq L \|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_j\|_2$.
    \item \textbf{(A2) Conditional Independence:} Task outcomes are conditionally independent given the context vectors.
    \item \textbf{(A3) Semi-bandit Feedback:} The platform observes the specific reward $Q(\mathbf{z}_{i,j}^t)$ only for the worker-task pairs actually selected.
    \item \textbf{(A4) Boundedness:} Rewards and costs are bounded, i.e., $Q(\mathbf{z}) \in [0, Q_{\max}]$ and $C_i^t \in [0, C_{\max}]$.
    \item \textbf{(A5) Feasibility:} We assume $N > M$ to ensure sufficient worker availability for feasible assignment.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Delayed Feedback Modeling}
\label{subsec:delayed_feedback}
In realistic edge computing scenarios, feedback is not instantaneous due to processing and transmission latencies. We denote the delay for a task assigned at time $t$ as $\tau_t$. The feedback $Q(\mathbf{z}_{i,j}^t)$ becomes available at $t + \tau_t$. Consequently, at time $t$, the history $\mathcal{H}_t$ contains only observations from tasks completed before $t$. This discrepancy creates a "missing data" challenge, requiring our algorithm to update policies based on partial information without halting the decision process.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
The objective is to maximize the platform's profit while satisfying system constraints. The optimization problem is formulated as:

\begin{align}
    \max_{\mathbf{x}} \quad & \Pi^t = \sum_{j=1}^{M} \sum_{i=1}^{N} x_{i,j}^t (R_j^t - C_i^t) \tag{1} \\
    \text{s.t.} \quad 
    & \sum_{j=1}^{M} x_{i,j}^t \leq 1, \quad \forall i  \tag{2} \\
    & \sum_{i=1}^{N} x_{i,j}^t = 1, \quad \forall j  \tag{3} \\
    & x_{i,j}^t \in \{0,1\}, \quad \forall i,j  \tag{4} \\
    & E_{i}^t \leq E_i^{\max}, \quad \forall i  \tag{5} \\
    & \sum_{t=1}^{T_j^{\max}} \sum_{i=1}^{N} x_{i,j}^t \cdot Q_{i,j}^t \geq Q_j^{\text{req}}, \quad \forall j  \tag{6} \\
    & \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{M} x_{i,j}^t \cdot D_j^t \leq B^{\max}. \quad \forall t \tag{7}
\end{align}

Constraints (2)-(4) ensure a valid one-to-one matching where each task is assigned to exactly one worker, and each worker handles at most one task. Constraint (5) enforces the worker's energy budget $E_i^{\max}$. Constraint (6) guarantees the cumulative quality requirement for tasks spanning multiple slots. Finally, Constraint (7) ensures the aggregate data transmission does not exceed the system bandwidth capacity $B^{\max}$.
